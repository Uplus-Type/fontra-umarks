U+Marks

Accented glyphs, built from anchors.

U+Marks builds the composed glyphs of your font — é, Ǻ, ệ — from the base letters and the accents you draw, placed by their anchors. You draw the parts once; U+Marks composes the rest, shows you what each anchor will receive while you move it, and tells you when a composed glyph no longer matches its anchors.

The panel reads top to bottom, in the order of the work. Each line says where you are and offers one action. No line blocks the next: what is ready is done, the rest waits.

① Glyph set — what you want to end up with

The menu lists the glyph sets you chose for this font; the first time it is empty. Add a glyph set… offers the glyph sets of your project, your own (the ones you added to Fontra), Fontra's presets (Google Fonts Latin Core, Adobe Latin, Koeberlin…), or From scratch. The line counts what concerns U+Marks: the glyphs to compose and the parts they need. The rest of the glyph set is none of its business.

Edit… lets you add or remove glyphs (type names, or the characters themselves), compose bases with marks of your own — see « Rules, and where they come from » below — or remove the glyph set from the menu (nothing is deleted in the font). The … menu exports and imports the rules.

② Draw — what is still missing

The parts the rules need and the font does not have yet, bases and accents. Create N empty makes them as empty glyphs with their code point, so they appear in the font overview and you can draw them in Fontra. The glyphs that exist but are empty in a source are listed under « still empty ».

Spacing accents (acute, grave, dieresis…) are not drawn: U+Marks makes them from the combining ones. See « Legacy accents ».

③ Anchors — put the anchors on what is drawn

Anchors are what U+Marks composes with: a base carries top, bottom, ogonek; a mark carries the matching _top, _bottom, _ogonek. The positions list says where each anchor goes — centre of the glyph at the x-height, baseline, cap height plus ten units — and the tick says whether it is placed. Place anchors adds the missing anchors to every drawn glyph of the glyph set, in every source, following the italic angle if you ask it to. It never moves an anchor that is already there.

To add or move one anchor by hand, use Fontra: right-click on the canvas, Add Anchor, then drag it. U+Marks reads what you did.

④ Check — are the anchors where they should be?

Check compares the anchors of the glyph set with their positions and lists the ones that are far from it, source by source. An anchor U+Marks placed and whose position has since changed (you edited the positions, or the glyph) is « out of date »: Re-place N puts it back. An anchor you moved yourself is « moved by hand » and is left alone unless you tick it.

Adjust holds the batch tools: Move, Align, Copy from, Mirror, Derive variants. One operation at a time; the sentence above Apply says exactly what will happen.

⑤ Compose — make the glyphs

Each composed glyph of the glyph set has a rule: a base, one or more marks, each on an anchor — eacute = e + acutecomb @ top. The line says how many are ready, how many wait for a part that is not drawn yet (and which), and how many are impossible without changing a rule or an anchor. Make N writes the ready ones: components only, the base at the origin, each mark placed anchor to anchor, the advance of the base, in every source.

A glyph that already has a drawing of its own is « drawn by hand ». Make N leaves it alone; tick it and a sheet shows the old and the new, and asks before overwriting.

⑥ Update — when the anchors have moved

Once a glyph is made, U+Marks keeps watching. Move top on e and every glyph built on it is « out of date »; the line says by how much and in which sources. Recompute N places the components again from the current anchors, and touches nothing else. A component you moved yourself counts too: the glyph tells you, and you choose — Recompute, or leave it.

The open glyph

Under the path, the glyph you are editing.

On a base: its ghosts — every accent a rule puts on it, drawn where it would sit, following the anchor as you drag it; the menu shows all of them or one. Then « Composes »: the glyphs built on this base, made ✓, to make ○, or possible but not in the glyph set — click one to add it.

On a composed glyph: its rule, editable; its state; the ghost is what Make would write, so an offset shows at once.

On an accent: the bases that use it, in ghost.

Rules, and where they come from

A rule is written for you from three sources, in this order:

  • Unicode. The canonical decomposition of each character of the glyph set: é is e + U+0301, so eacute = e + acutecomb @ top. Stacked marks follow (ǘ = u + diaeresis + acute). Soft-dotted letters take their dotless twin for a mark above: imacron = idotless + macroncomb.
  • Your font. A composed glyph that already exists says its own rule: Aacute built from A and acute.cap reads as that, and is « made ».
  • Your anchors. For a glyph Unicode does not know, any base with top and any mark with _top can be composed. On the open glyph, « From its anchors » lists them; one click writes the rule. In Edit…, « Compose these bases with these marks » writes a whole set at once, named base_mark unless you say otherwise.

A rule written for you is marked auto. Edit it, or press Keep, and it becomes yours: U+Marks never changes it again. Auto rules follow the font — draw acutecomb.cap and the capitals pick it up, and ⑥ says which glyphs to recompute.

Accents by category: a capital takes acute.cap when the font has it, a small capital acute.sc; the table is in the rule's menu. Anchors top.cap/_top.cap work too, for fonts that prefer one accent drawing placed lower on capitals.

Legacy accents

The spacing accents of Latin-1 — acute, grave, dieresis… — are not drawn. Each gets a rule of its own kind: one component of the combining accent, with an advance computed from the font (the width of the accent plus two equal margins, the margin of your o unless you set another in the settings). They go through ⑤ and ⑥ like everything else.

Undo

Every batch — Create, Place, Re-place, Make, Recompute, Adjust, the matrix — is remembered as one. Undo last batch takes the last one back, glyph by glyph, and leaves what you touched since, saying so. Fontra's own ⌘Z still works, one glyph at a time.

Sharing the rules

The rules, the glyph sets you chose, the anchor positions and the settings live in the font (customData, under umarks), so your collaborators see the same list. The … menu of ① exports them as a JSON file, to carry a family's rules to the next, and imports one — yours, or an old MakeAccents file — without ever replacing a rule you wrote by hand.

On Fontra Pak or your own Fontra server

Settings › Plugins, add the address Uplus-Type/fontra-umarks. Fontra loads the latest release from the public repository; nothing to install. In Fontra Hive it is already there.
